1 Hour Uphill Grounding Hike: 3 In, 4 Out on Any Terrain | hikingIQ | Sportstechwest

hikingIQ: week two. Same grounding practice (heel-toe steps, breathe in over 3, out over 4), now taken uphill and back down.

Most people meet a climb by rushing and holding their breath. This session does the opposite: shorter steps, the same count, and a longer exhale to keep your head clear when the trail gets steep.

The hike:
• Flat warm-up to lock in the 3-in, 4-out rhythm
• The climb: half-length steps, lean from the ankles, drop to 2 in and 3 out if you need to
• The summit: stand on both heels and take it in
• The descent: soft knees, quiet feet, control over speed
• Quiet stretches and a cool-down

Short affirmations along the way: I climb at my own pace. I bring the ground with me.

Know your route, hike somewhere safe, and keep one ear on the trail.

Why it works: breathing naturally syncs with the rhythm of your steps (Bramble & Carrier, 1983). Keeping the exhale long on a climb keeps you closer to the slow, controlled breathing linked to calm and focus (Zaccaro et al., 2018).
