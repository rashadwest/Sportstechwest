**Thread (optional):**
1. Lance Stephenson vs. Michael Beasley wasn't just a viral clip. It was a lesson on how skill actually works. 🧵
2. Beasley practiced defending chokes. Didn't matter. A drill against a cooperative partner and a live opponent are different data.
3. Research calls it representative learning design: practice only transfers if it keeps the real pressures of competition.
4. Pressure also changes HOW skill runs. Under stress we start monitoring every step, and smooth skills break down. That's choking.
5. The fix: bring small doses of pressure into practice. Athletes who train under anxiety hold up better under pressure.
6. Try it: 20 FTs fresh vs. 20 FTs tired with a consequence on every miss. Practice % − Pressure % = your Pressure Gap. Track it weekly.
7. Full breakdown, plus the BIG3 epilogue and the attention-economy angle: https://sportstechwest.com/blog/practicing-is-not-pressure-beasley-vs-stephenson/
