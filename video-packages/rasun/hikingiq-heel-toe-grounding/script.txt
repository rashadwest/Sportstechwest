# hikingIQ: Heel-Toe Grounding (1-hour guided hike)
# Pattern: breathe in over 3 heel-to-toe steps, breathe out over 4 heel-to-toe steps. Repeat.
# Same format as the walking/IQ sessions: one segment per paragraph, 5 seconds of quiet after each.
# "[hike N]" = about N seconds of music-only hiking instead of the 5-second gap (scaled to fill 60:00).

This is hikingIQ. One hour on the trail. One way to stay grounded. Heel, then toe.

Hike somewhere you know, or with someone who does. Keep one ear open to the trail around you.

Here's the practice. Every step lands heel first, then rolls through to the toe. Heel. Toe. Feel the ground take your weight.

Now pair it with your breath. Breathe in over three heel-to-toe steps. Breathe out over four heel-to-toe steps.

In, for three steps. Out, for four steps. The longer exhale is the point. It tells your body to settle.

Start slow. Find the heel. Find the toe. Don't count the breath yet. Just feel each step land.
[hike 45]

Now add the breath. In for three steps. Heel, toe. Heel, toe. Heel, toe. Out for four steps. Heel, toe, four times.

If you lose the count, wait for your next heel to touch down, and start again at one.
[hike 90]

Notice the ground under you. Dirt, rock, roots, leaves. Every surface feels a little different through the heel.

I am here. I am grounded.
[hike 60]

Let your arms swing loose. Let your shoulders drop. The trail isn't going anywhere.

In for three. Out for four. Keep it going on your own for the next few minutes.
[hike 90]

Grounding means your attention lives in your body and your feet, not in your inbox or tomorrow.

When a thought pulls you away, that's fine. Thank it, let it pass, and come back to heel, toe.
[hike 90]

Every step brings me back.
[hike 75]

Soften your eyes. Look a few steps ahead, not at your phone and not at the top of the hill.

Feel the heel strike. Feel the roll. Feel the push through the toe. Three steps in. Four steps out.
[hike 90]

I don't have to rush. The trail will wait for me.
[hike 75]

If the path tilts uphill, shorten your steps. Keep the same count. Three in. Four out. Smaller steps, same rhythm.
[hike 90]

If the path tilts down, soften your knees. Let the heel land gently. Control beats speed going down.

I move with the ground, not against it.
[hike 90]

Check in. Jaw loose. Hands relaxed. Breath low in the belly.
[hike 90]

Notice one sound on the trail right now. Wind. Birds. Your own footsteps. Let it sit in the background while you keep counting.

I am calm. I am steady. I am grounded.
[hike 90]

This is what grounding does. It turns down the noise so you can hear yourself again.
[hike 90]

Back to the count. In for three. Out for four. Heel, toe.

I trust my feet.
[hike 120]

Feel the air on your face. Cool or warm, it's real, and it's right now.
[hike 120]

The trail asks for one thing at a time. This step. Then the next one.

I take life one step at a time.
[hike 120]

One long quiet stretch now. No new instructions. Just heel, toe, three in, four out.
[hike 150]

Still with it. Good.

I leave the trail lighter than I found it.
[hike 120]

Let's start to slow down. Easy steps. Still heel first. Still the count.

Try one breath with a longer exhale. In for three steps. Out for five, if it feels good.
[hike 75]

Your heart is slowing because you told it to. You practiced that, one step at a time, for an hour.

I am here. I am grounded. Every step brings me back.
[hike 60]

Take three more breaths on the count. Then let the counting go, and just walk.
[hike 45]

That's hikingIQ. Heel, toe. Three in, four out. Bring it to your next hike, and the one after that.
