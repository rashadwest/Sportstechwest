# hikingIQ: Grounding on the Climb (1-hour guided hike, week 2)
# Same breath pattern: in over 3 heel-to-toe steps, out over 4. This one is built around terrain: flat, uphill, downhill.
# One segment per paragraph, 5 seconds of quiet after each. "[hike N]" = music-only stretch (scaled to fill 60:00).

## Flat warm-up
This is hikingIQ. Last week we found the ground. This week we take it uphill.

Same rule. Hike somewhere safe, know your route, and keep one ear on the trail.

Quick reset if you're new. Each step lands heel first, then rolls to the toe. Breathe in over three steps. Breathe out over four.

Three in. Four out. The long exhale keeps you calm when the trail gets steep.

Start on the flat. Heel, toe. Find the count again.
[hike 60]

In for three. Out for four. Let it get automatic before the climb shows up.
[hike 90]

I bring the ground with me.
[hike 60]

Think about the hill ahead of you, whether it's real or not. Most people meet a climb by rushing and holding their breath. We're going to do the opposite.

## The climb
When the trail tilts up, shorten your step. Half the length. Same count. Three in, four out.
[hike 90]

Short steps on a climb aren't weakness. They're how you keep your breath, and your breath is how you keep your head.

Heel down. Roll through. Push off the toe. Let the legs do the climbing, not your shoulders.
[hike 90]

I climb at my own pace.
[hike 75]

If three and four feels tight on the steep part, go to two in and three out. Keep the exhale longer. Come back to three and four when it eases.
[hike 90]

Lean slightly forward from the ankles, not the waist. Eyes up the trail. Chest open so the breath has room.

I can be breathing hard and still be grounded.
[hike 90]

This is the skill. Effort goes up, and your attention stays on the step under you.
[hike 90]

A climb is just a lot of single steps. You only ever have to take this one.

One step. Then the next.
[hike 90]

## The summit
Reach the top. Stop for a moment if you want. Stand with both heels on the ground. Feel how solid it is.
[hike 60]

Look around. Take in the view for a few breaths. You earned it, one step at a time.

I am proud of the work I put in.
[hike 90]

## The descent
Now the way down. Downhill is where most slips happen, so this part matters.

Soften your knees. Shorter steps again. Let the heel land gently, then roll to the toe. Three in, four out.
[hike 90]

Control beats speed going down. Quiet feet. Soft knees. Steady breath.

I move with the ground, not against it.
[hike 90]

If you hear your feet slapping, slow down. Grounded steps are quiet steps.
[hike 120]

Back on the flat. Let your stride open up again. Same count.

I trust my legs on any terrain.
[hike 120]

## Quiet stretches and cool-down
One long quiet stretch. Just the trail, your feet, and three in, four out.
[hike 150]

Notice what's around you. Trees, rocks, sky. All of it was here long before the climb, and it'll be here after.

I am part of this place.
[hike 120]

Start to ease off. Easy steps. Longer exhales. In for three, out for five if it feels good.
[hike 75]

Uphill, downhill, or flat, the rhythm stayed with you. That's grounding you can use anywhere.

I bring the ground with me.
[hike 60]

Take three more breaths on the count, then let it go and just walk.
[hike 45]

That's hikingIQ. Three in, four out, on any hill. Take it to your next trail.
